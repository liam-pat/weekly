## Learning 

**Mindfulness**  
Notes from a book I read—there are many ways to meditate:  

Meditation is about filtering out society's noise to listen to your own inner voice.  
Meditation only works when done for the sake of meditation itself.  
Hiking -> Walking meditation.  
Journaling -> Writing meditation.  
Taking a shower -> Accidental meditation.  
Sitting quietly -> Direct meditation.  

**Notes**  
Found these in my notes, no idea where they originated, but copying down a few lines I liked:  

- Break away from the average person's **thinking habits**; don't just **go with the flow**, even if those around you misunderstand, isolate, or mock you.  
- Detach from average **habits** and **social circles**.  
- Avoid unnecessary **consumption** and **debt**.  
- Learn to think deeply in solitude with zero distractions. Focus solely on your own learning and growth. (Leverage mentors and benefactors).  
- Learn to build reserves, don't compare yourself to others, only buy essentials.  
- Avoid a purely speculative mindset; '24 is not the time for reckless entrepreneurship.  

**The 5 Principles**  

1. The Holistic Principle: **Big-picture view**. Don't learn just for the sake of learning—integrate new ideas with your past knowledge to synthesize understanding.  
2. The Focus Principle: Learning should have **clear priorities** and specific goals.  
3. The Challenge Principle: Goals should be **challenging** and appropriately demanding—don't just skim the surface.  
4. The Feasibility Principle: **Grounded in reality**—don't set goals way outside your current capabilities.  
5. The Adaptability Principle: Goals must be flexible and reasonably adjustable. Learning a skill isn't just for one single objective, but to add another versatile tool to your belt.
